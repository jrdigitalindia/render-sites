Hydration, Dry Eyes and Everyday Comfort

OptiPlax Eye Wellness Journal · 2026-10-21

Dry, scratchy eyes by the end of the day are often blamed on screens alone, but hydration plays a bigger supporting role than most people realize. The surface of the eye relies on a thin tear film to stay comfortable, and overall hydration status is one of several factors that can influence how well that tear film holds up through a long day.

The Everyday Connection

You don't need to drastically overhaul your water intake to notice a difference — the goal is simply consistency through the day rather than catching up all at once in the evening. Pairing a glass of water with other daily anchors (a meal, a work break, a 20-20-20 reset) tends to work better than relying on willpower alone.

Environmental Factors That Compound Dryness

  • Air conditioning and heating, which both reduce ambient humidity
  • Extended screen use, which reduces natural blink rate — covered in our digital eye strain guide
  • Poor sleep, which can leave eyes feeling drier and more irritated the next day — see our piece on sleep and your eyes

A Simple Daily Check

If your eyes consistently feel dry by mid-afternoon, it's worth looking at hydration, screen habits and indoor air quality together rather than any one in isolation — they tend to compound. And if dryness is persistent or severe, it's worth having it looked at by an eye-care professional rather than managing it with habits alone.
