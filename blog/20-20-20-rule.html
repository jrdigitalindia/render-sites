The 20-20-20 Rule and Other Simple Eye-Break Habits

OptiPlax Eye Wellness Journal · 2026-09-23

Of all the habits recommended for screen-heavy days, the 20-20-20 rule is probably the most widely repeated — and for good reason: it's genuinely simple. Every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds. That's it.

Why It Works

Prolonged close-up focus — the kind involved in reading a screen a couple feet from your face — keeps the eye's focusing muscles in a sustained, static position. Briefly shifting focus to something farther away gives those muscles a short reset, similar to standing up and stretching during a long stretch of sitting.

Habits Worth Pairing With It

  • Deliberate blinking — a few slow, full blinks every so often helps counter the reduced blink rate that comes with screen focus, which ties into broader digital eye strain habits
  • A short walk — even two minutes away from your desk combines a visual break with light movement
  • Screen position checks — keeping your screen slightly below eye level and about an arm's length away reduces strain compared to screens that are too close or too high

Making It Stick

The hardest part of the 20-20-20 rule isn't the rule itself — it's remembering to do it. A recurring phone reminder, a sticky note on your monitor, or simply pairing it with something else you already do regularly (like finishing a cup of coffee) can help it become automatic. It's a small habit, but it's one of the easier pieces to slot into a broader daily eye wellness routine, alongside nutrition and consistent sleep.
