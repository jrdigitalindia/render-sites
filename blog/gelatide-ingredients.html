Gelatide Ingredients: A Closer Look at All 6 Botanicals

Published November 7, 2026 · 9 min read

Understanding a supplement becomes much easier when you know exactly what each ingredient is and how well-studied it actually is. Gelatide names six botanicals on its label — no vague "proprietary blend" hiding the details, though no milligram amount is printed for any of them either. Here's a closer look at each one, evidence level included.

1. Green Tea Leaf — Camellia sinensis

The catechin and caffeine source in the formula. This is the deepest trial record of the six, typically studied at 270-1,200mg of catechins daily, though research on appetite hormones specifically hasn't shown a consistent effect. Evidence level: Moderate — well-studied for metabolism generally, not for appetite specifically.

2. Guarana Seed — Paullinia cupana

An Amazonian seed carrying two to four times the caffeine of coffee bean by weight, typically studied at 50-75mg of dried extract daily. It has its own genuine fatigue-reduction research, and some evidence it outperforms a matched dose of plain caffeine. Evidence level: Moderate — real energy research, but not a weight or appetite ingredient on its own.

3. African Mango Seed — Irvingia gabonensis

A West African seed extract that carries much of the appetite-control marketing in this product category, typically studied at 150mg twice daily. Its supporting trials were judged small, short and unconvincing on systematic review. Evidence level: Weak — didn't hold up well under closer review.

4. Coleus Forskohlii Root

The source of forskolin, which raises cyclic AMP in cells — the mechanism behind the "fat metabolism" claim. Typically studied at 250mg of a 10% extract, twice daily. Evidence level: Mixed — a well-established cellular mechanism, with human trials showing body-composition shifts but not consistent overall weight change.

5. Capsicum Annuum Fruit

Red pepper extract, the capsaicinoid source, typically studied at 0.9-10mg of capsaicinoids daily. This is the one ingredient on the label with a meta-analysis aimed directly at food intake, showing roughly 74 fewer kilocalories per meal. Evidence level: Strongest — the most specific, measured finding across all six botanicals.

6. Raspberry Ketones

The aroma compound responsible for raspberry scent. A long-standing category inclusion, but no human trial of the isolated compound has ever been run. Evidence level: None — its inclusion rests on tradition, not research.

How the Six Botanicals Are Organized

Looking at the formula as a whole, three broad categories explain the ingredient selection. Read the full breakdown in our how does Gelatide work article:

  • Digestion and metabolism — Green tea leaf and capsicum
  • Energy and fat metabolism — Guarana seed and Coleus forskohlii
  • Traditional appetite support — African mango seed and raspberry ketones

Why Evidence Transparency Matters

The supplement market is full of long ingredient lists and attractive promotional claims. A clear understanding of how strong the evidence actually is for each ingredient — not just whether it's named — helps you evaluate whether a formula is appropriate for your needs. Gelatide names its six ingredients plainly, but the evidence behind them varies considerably, from capsicum's genuine measured effect to raspberry ketone's complete absence of human trials.
