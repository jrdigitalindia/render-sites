Understanding Digital Eye Strain and How to Ease It

OptiPlax Eye Wellness Journal · 2026-09-02

If your eyes feel tired, dry or unfocused by mid-afternoon, you're not imagining it. Digital eye strain — sometimes called computer vision syndrome — is one of the most common complaints among anyone who spends long stretches looking at a screen, and modern work and home life make it almost unavoidable.

What's Actually Happening

When you look at a screen, you blink less often than you do during other activities — sometimes by half. Less blinking means less natural moisture across the surface of the eye, which is part of why screens leave eyes feeling dry and gritty by the end of the day. Add in glare, small text and close viewing distances, and the eye muscles responsible for focus end up working harder than usual for hours at a stretch.

Simple Habits That Help

A few small adjustments can make a noticeable difference. The 20-20-20 rule is one of the simplest — every 20 minutes, look at something roughly 20 feet away for 20 seconds. It gives the focusing muscles in your eyes a short, regular break.

Lighting matters too. Position screens to reduce glare from windows or overhead lights, and keep screen brightness roughly matched to the room around you — a screen that's much brighter or dimmer than its surroundings forces your eyes to work harder to adjust.

Hydration plays a role as well; see our piece on hydration and dry eyes for more on that connection. And because a lot of eye strain shows up most at the end of a long day, it's worth considering how the rest of your daily routine — including sleep — supports your eyes overall.

Building It Into a Routine

None of these habits need to be complicated. The goal is consistency, not perfection — a few deliberate breaks through the day, a bit more attention to lighting, and a daily routine that treats eye wellness as one part of a bigger picture. For a broader look at putting that routine together, see building an eye wellness routine that actually sticks.

If digital eye strain is persistent, significantly affects your vision, or doesn't improve with these habits, it's worth having it checked out by an eye-care professional — supplements and daily habits are a complement to professional care, not a substitute for it.
