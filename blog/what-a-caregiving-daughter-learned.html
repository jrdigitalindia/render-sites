What I Learned Helping My Mom Stay Sharp

The moment I noticed something had changed

It wasn't dramatic. My mom, 68 at the time, called me twice in one afternoon asking the same question about a family dinner. On its own, that's nothing — everyone forgets things. But it kept happening, small and quiet, over a few months. That's usually how it goes, from what I've since learned: not one big moment, but a pattern you only notice in hindsight.

What we tried, and what actually stuck

We talked to her doctor first, which I'd recommend to anyone in a similar spot — ruling out anything serious matters more than any supplement or lifestyle change. Once that conversation happened, we looked at her daily routine together: sleep, walking, staying in touch with friends, and eventually, a cognitive support supplement she could take alongside all of it.

What actually stuck wasn't anything dramatic. It was simple enough that she didn't need reminding — two capsules with her morning coffee, a short walk most days, and a standing Sunday call with her sister that got her talking through her week out loud.

The routine that fit her life

She's been taking Mind Vault daily for a while now, alongside those other habits. I won't pretend I can separate out exactly what's doing what — that's not how this works, and I'd rather be honest about that than oversell it. What I can say is that around the two-to-three month mark, she seemed more present on our calls, quicker to pick the conversation back up after a pause. Slow, steady, and real.

What I tell other families now

Start with the doctor's visit. Then build a routine simple enough that your parent will actually keep doing it — the best plan is the one that survives a bad week. And go in with realistic expectations: this is a gradual process, not a light switch.
