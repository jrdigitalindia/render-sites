Cayenne Pepper Extract

The heat compound, with genuine thermogenesis research behind it and a digestive caveat worth planning for.

Why Cayenne Pepper Is in SlimTide

The source of capsaicin, the compound behind chilli heat, studied for small effects on energy expenditure and appetite. It is one of the six named ingredients in the SlimTide formula, and this page covers what it is, the general research context around it, its role here and the cautions attached to it.

SlimTide does not publish its ingredient amounts

All six ingredients are named, which is more than many products in this category manage. What is not published anywhere on the official source is how much of each one a capsule contains.

That gap matters, because effect depends on amount. Chromium has a defined daily requirement measured in micrograms. Garcinia trials used more than a gram of hydroxycitric acid daily. L-carnitine research typically used one to three grams. Without figures, none of it can be compared to what is actually in the bottle, so no milligram amounts appear anywhere on this website — inventing them would be worse than leaving them out.

What Cayenne Pepper Extract in SlimTide Is

Cayenne pepper extract is derived from chilli peppers of the Capsicum annuum family. Its active compounds are capsaicinoids, principally capsaicin, which is what produces the burning sensation of chilli heat.

That sensation is not damage. Capsaicin binds to a receptor called TRPV1 that normally responds to actual heat, which is why the brain interprets it as burning even though nothing is being burned.

The reference describes the extract as standardised for consistent potency, though it does not publish the standardisation percentage or the amount per capsule.

The Research Context Behind Cayenne in SlimTide

Capsaicin has a real, if modest, research base in this area, and it is one of the more defensible inclusions in the formula.

Studies have examined its effects on thermogenesis — the energy the body expends producing heat — with several finding small increases in energy expenditure after capsaicin intake. Meta-analyses have generally concluded the effect is real but small.

There is also research on appetite. Some studies have reported reduced energy intake at a subsequent meal after capsaicin consumption, and reduced desire for fatty or salty foods.

The honest framing: measured effects tend to be on the order of tens of calories a day, which is genuine but easily offset by minor variations in eating. Tolerance also appears to develop with regular consumption.

Why Cayenne Is Included in SlimTide

It carries part of the formula's thermogenic pathway, alongside green tea. The two are frequently combined because they act through different mechanisms.

The amount is not published. That matters here because capsaicin's effects are dose-related and because tolerability is dose-related too — the difference between a comfortable dose and an uncomfortable one is not large for some people.

Responsible Expectations and Safety for Cayenne in SlimTide

Expect a small metabolic contribution rather than something you would notice. Capsaicin is a supporting ingredient in a formula, not a mechanism for weight change.

Safety notes. The most common issues are digestive: a burning sensation, heartburn, stomach discomfort or nausea, particularly when taken on an empty stomach. Taking the capsule with food often helps, and is worth trying if the first few days are uncomfortable.

Anyone with acid reflux, gastritis, an ulcer or irritable bowel symptoms should be cautious, since capsaicin can aggravate all of them. Capsaicin may also have mild blood-thinning properties, so mention it to your doctor if you take anticoagulant medication or have surgery planned.

How to read the research on this SlimTide page

The studies described here examined individual ingredients, usually on their own, at doses set by the researchers. That research provides scientific context for why an ingredient appears in a formula.

It does not demonstrate that the finished SlimTide product produces the same outcomes. No clinical trial of this finished formula has been published, so nothing here is product-level clinical evidence.
