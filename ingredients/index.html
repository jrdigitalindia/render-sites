How the SlimTide Formula Groups Together

Six ingredients across four pathways. Reading them grouped by evidence tells you considerably more than taking them in the order printed on the page.

Established nutrition

Chromium is the only ingredient here that is an essential trace mineral — something the body genuinely requires, with a defined daily intake and a real role in insulin function. Its status is settled; the open question is whether supplementing helps people who are not deficient.

Reasonable research bases

Green tea leaf extract has a substantial literature on catechins, thermogenesis and fat oxidation, with consistently small effects. Alpha lipoic acid has genuine clinical research, though the strongest of it concerns diabetic neuropathy rather than weight management.

Plausible mechanisms, modest human evidence

L-carnitine's role in transporting fatty acids into mitochondria is textbook biochemistry, but supplementation trials in non-deficient people have been mixed. Cayenne pepper extract's capsaicin has real but small studies on energy expenditure and appetite.

The most studied and least convincing

Garcinia cambogia has been examined more than most ingredients in this category and reviewed unfavourably. The most-cited systematic review concluded the effect on body weight was small and of questionable clinical relevance.

SlimTide does not publish its ingredient amounts

All six ingredients are named, which is more than many products in this category manage. What is not published anywhere on the official source is how much of each one a capsule contains.

That gap matters, because effect depends on amount. Chromium has a defined daily requirement measured in micrograms. Garcinia trials used more than a gram of hydroxycitric acid daily. L-carnitine research typically used one to three grams. Without figures, none of it can be compared to what is actually in the bottle, so no milligram amounts appear anywhere on this website — inventing them would be worse than leaving them out.

How to read the research on this SlimTide page

The studies described here examined individual ingredients, usually on their own, at doses set by the researchers. That research provides scientific context for why an ingredient appears in a formula.

It does not demonstrate that the finished SlimTide product produces the same outcomes. No clinical trial of this finished formula has been published, so nothing here is product-level clinical evidence.
