Catechins and caffeine

Green Tea Leaf Extract

The formula's best-researched thermogenic ingredient — and the source of its caffeine, which the seller flags directly.

Why Green Tea Extract Is in SlimTide

A standardised leaf extract supplying catechins including EGCG, plus a natural dose of caffeine the reference itself flags. It is one of the six named ingredients in the SlimTide formula, and this page covers what it is, the general research context around it, its role here and the cautions attached to it.

SlimTide does not publish its ingredient amounts

All six ingredients are named, which is more than many products in this category manage. What is not published anywhere on the official source is how much of each one a capsule contains.

That gap matters, because effect depends on amount. Chromium has a defined daily requirement measured in micrograms. Garcinia trials used more than a gram of hydroxycitric acid daily. L-carnitine research typically used one to three grams. Without figures, none of it can be compared to what is actually in the bottle, so no milligram amounts appear anywhere on this website — inventing them would be worse than leaving them out.

What Green Tea Leaf Extract in SlimTide Is

Green tea leaf extract is a concentrated preparation from Camellia sinensis leaves — the same plant as ordinary green tea, with the polyphenols concentrated.

The compounds of interest are catechins, particularly epigallocatechin gallate, usually written EGCG. The reference describes the extract as sourced from organic farms and standardised for potency, though it does not publish the standardisation percentage or the amount.

Green tea extract also contains caffeine, and the reference is refreshingly direct about it — its FAQ advises anyone sensitive to stimulants to leave about an hour between the capsule and their coffee.

The Research Context Behind Green Tea in SlimTide

This is the ingredient in the formula with the most substantial research base for weight management specifically.

The proposed mechanism is that catechins inhibit an enzyme that breaks down noradrenaline, prolonging its thermogenic signalling. This appears to work synergistically with caffeine, which is one reason the two are so often studied together — and why finding both in one extract is convenient.

Meta-analyses of catechin-plus-caffeine combinations have found small reductions in body weight and modest increases in energy expenditure and fat oxidation. The effects are consistent enough to be real and small enough to be easily offset by minor dietary variation.

One consistent finding worth knowing: the effects appear less pronounced in habitual caffeine consumers. If you already drink several coffees a day, expect less from this ingredient than the studies suggest.

Why Green Tea Is Included in SlimTide

It supplies both the antioxidant element and the strongest thermogenic evidence in the formula, and it does so in a single ingredient that brings its own caffeine.

The reference notes green tea's excellent safety profile in clinical studies, which is broadly fair at moderate doses.

The amount is not published, and it matters in both directions here. Most studies showing thermogenic effects used substantial catechin intakes — frequently several hundred milligrams of EGCG daily. At the same time, very high concentrated doses carry their own concern, described below.

Responsible Expectations and Safety for Green Tea in SlimTide

Expect a modest thermogenic and antioxidant contribution, less noticeable if you already consume caffeine regularly. Drinking green tea is a perfectly reasonable alternative source.

Safety notes. High-dose concentrated green tea extract has been associated with liver injury in a number of case reports, and European food safety authorities have flagged concerns at daily EGCG intakes of 800 mg and above from concentrated extracts. Brewed tea has not been associated with the same concern. Taking extracts with food rather than on an empty stomach is generally advised.

Green tea can reduce absorption of non-haem iron, which matters if you are managing low iron. It may interact with anticoagulant medication, and its caffeine content is a consideration for anyone sensitive to stimulants, pregnant or nursing, or managing blood pressure. Anyone with liver disease should avoid concentrated green tea extracts without medical advice.

How to read the research on this SlimTide page

The studies described here examined individual ingredients, usually on their own, at doses set by the researchers. That research provides scientific context for why an ingredient appears in a formula.

It does not demonstrate that the finished SlimTide product produces the same outcomes. No clinical trial of this finished formula has been published, so nothing here is product-level clinical evidence.

SlimTide contains caffeine, and two ingredients worth checking

Green tea extract supplies caffeine. The reference confirms this and advises anyone sensitive to stimulants to leave an hour between the capsule and their coffee. The amount is not published, so treat it as an unknown addition to your daily caffeine total and take the capsule in the morning.

Cayenne pepper extract commonly causes a burning sensation, heartburn or stomach upset, particularly on an empty stomach.

Chromium and alpha lipoic acid can both affect blood sugar, which matters a great deal if you take insulin or other diabetes medication — the combination can push glucose lower than intended. Alpha lipoic acid may also affect thyroid medication.

The reference's own advice is to consult a licensed doctor before taking any supplement, especially if you are pregnant, nursing, taking medication or managing a health condition. That is worth following rather than skimming.
